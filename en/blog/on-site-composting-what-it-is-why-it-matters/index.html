On-site composting: what it is and why it matters

"On-site composting" means treating organic waste right where it's generated, instead of shipping it to a landfill or an off-site composting facility. It's a simple change to the process, but with a significant impact.

How does it work?

An industrial electromechanical composter, like those from Iruña Composting, combines aeration, mixing and controlled heating to speed up the natural decomposition process. Within days, organic waste turns into a stable pre-compost, reducing its volume by up to 80-90%.

Why is adoption growing?

  • Regulatory pressure: more municipalities and regulations are restricting organic waste disposal in landfills.
  • Rising collection costs: on-site composting directly reduces the volume that needs to be collected and transported.
  • Sustainability commitments: hotels, restaurant chains and retailers need tangible evidence of their environmental management for customers and certifications.

Who is using it?

In Mexico, early adopters tend to be hotels, restaurants, supermarkets, institutional cafeterias and municipal governments -operations with a steady, significant volume of kitchen and garden waste.

How to choose the right equipment

The key is sizing the composter to your daily waste volume. That's why Iruña composters come in different capacities, from the IC-10 (35 kg/day) to the IC-100 (250 kg/day), covering everything from a small restaurant to a shopping center or municipal government.
